Sheet 1 of 5 · Controls Lead

Autonomous Drone

A Crazyflie quadcopter programmed in C++ for ME 136, Dynamics and Control of Autonomous Flight. Our team built it up from motor maps to closed-loop hover, then flew an autonomous mission: a 50 cm altitude hold and a 1 m lateral move, within ±5 cm. As controls lead, I worked on state estimation and optical flow sensing.

How it estimates its state

  • Attitude: a complementary filter fusing gyro and accelerometer data, with gyro bias removal.
  • Horizontal velocity: optical flow, corrected for rotation and scaled by height.
  • Height: a distance sensor fused with accelerometer-based vertical velocity.

Sheet 4 of 5 · ME 100 team project

Solar Shade

An automated window blind built by a three-person team. A solar cell senses sunlight, and an ESP32 opens or closes the blinds on its own to keep rooms cooler. It works without Wi-Fi, and IFTTT sends status alerts.

How it works

  • Sensing: a solar cell read by an INA219, plus a VEML7700 light sensor.
  • Control: the ESP32 runs the motor when solar voltage crosses a threshold.
  • Actuation: a 6 V micromotor on a DRV8833 H-bridge turns the blind rod.
  • Data: readings are logged to Adafruit IO, and alerts go through IFTTT.

Key challenges

  • Controlling the motor's speed and travel.
  • Designing the geometry for opening and closing the blinds.
  • Keeping the code from repeating a command, which jammed the motor.
  • Sending data for live plots without overwhelming the wireless control signal.
